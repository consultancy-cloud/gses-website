What we resolve

The problems an inspection actually solves.

These are the situations homeowners bring us most often. If yours isn't listed, it is almost certainly a variation of one of them — call us and we'll tell you honestly whether an inspection will help.

Performance

Your bill didn't drop the way you were promised

The system is "working" but the savings never appeared. Common causes: undersized or badly configured inverter, strings wired to the wrong MPPT, shading nobody modelled, export limits set to protect the network rather than you, or a quoted yield that was never achievable.

What we do: measure actual output against what the site can genuinely produce, and identify which of those causes applies.
Faults

Inverter errors, dropouts and dead strings

Repeat fault codes, an inverter that shuts down in the middle of the day, or one section of the array producing nothing. Usually an isolator, connector, arc-fault or earthing problem rather than the inverter itself — but you get told to replace the inverter.

What we do: trace the fault to the actual component, so you replace the right thing once.
Safety

Water in the DC isolator and corroded rooftop gear

One of the most frequent defects found nationally. Rooftop isolator enclosures that fail their IP rating let water in, corrode the switch and create a real fire risk — often years before anyone notices, because the system keeps producing.

What we do: open and inspect every isolation point, document the condition and rate the urgency.
Compliance

Undersized or wrongly rated DC protection

DC circuit-breaker rating and sizing is one of the defects we report on most. Get it wrong and the device that is meant to protect the array can't do its job under fault conditions — and your insurer may have something to say about it.

What we do: verify protection device sizing and cable ratings against the installed array and AS/NZS 5033.
Safety

Earthing and equipotential bonding that isn't there

Functional earthing and bonding of frames and rails is routinely skipped or done badly. It is invisible from the ground, it doesn't stop the system generating, and it is exactly the sort of thing that only surfaces after an incident.

What we do: physically verify earthing continuity and bonding across the array and mounting system.
Battery

Is my home battery actually safe where it's installed?

Batteries have hard rules about location, clearances, fire separation, ventilation and signage under AS/NZS 5139. Garage walls shared with a bedroom, units mounted under stairs or beside exit paths — these are not edge cases, we find them regularly.

What we do: assess siting, clearances, protection and documentation against AS/NZS 5139 — the standard GSES helped author.
Disputes

The installer has gone quiet on your warranty claim

Defect reported, nobody comes back. Or the installer has folded and the manufacturer wants "evidence of a compliant installation" before they'll honour anything. Without an independent report you're arguing opinion against opinion.

What we do: produce the independent, standards-referenced evidence a warranty claim, insurer or tribunal will accept.
Notices

You've received a defect notice or failed an inspection

A network, regulator or program inspection has flagged your system and you don't know how serious it is, whether the quoted rectification is fair, or whether the work done since actually closed the finding.

What we do: translate the notice, confirm what genuinely needs fixing, and verify rectification once it's done.
Property

Buying or selling a home with solar or a battery

A ten-year-old array with no paperwork is a liability, not a feature. Buyers inherit the defects; sellers get the price knocked down over uncertainty. A building inspector will not open a DC isolator.

What we do: a condition and compliance assessment of the energy system, so the asset can be priced honestly.
Roof

Leaks, lifted tiles and mounting you're unsure about

Penetrations that were never flashed properly, rails fixed to battens instead of rafters, or an array that predates the current wind-loading requirements for your area. Water and wind find these faster than you will.

What we do: inspect mounting, fixings and penetrations, and flag structural concerns for a structural engineer where needed.
EV

Your EV charger trips the house or was never signed off

Home chargers get installed on circuits that can't carry them, without load management, RCD protection of the right type, or the paperwork the network required. It works until the day it doesn't.

What we do: verify the charger installation, protection and supply capacity — we run EV charger inspection programs for government.
Paperwork

You have no documentation for a system you paid for

No compliance certificate, no shutdown procedure, no as-built diagram, no commissioning data. That paperwork is a legal requirement of the installation — and it is the first thing every insurer, buyer and manufacturer asks for.

What we do: audit what exists, document what the installed system actually is, and give you a complete record.
The inspection

What we actually look at.

01

Array & mounting

  • Module condition, damage and degradation
  • Rail, clamp and fixing integrity
  • Roof penetrations and weatherproofing
  • Orientation, tilt and shading against yield
  • Earthing and equipotential bonding
02

DC side

  • Isolator enclosures, IP rating and water ingress
  • DC protection device rating and sizing
  • Cable selection, support and UV exposure
  • Connector type-matching and terminations
  • String configuration and polarity
03

Inverter & switchboard

  • Inverter mounting, ventilation and fault history
  • AS/NZS 4777 grid settings and export limits
  • Switchboard labelling and signage
  • AC protection, RCDs and main switch arrangement
  • Metering and monitoring configuration
04

Battery storage

  • Location, clearances and fire separation
  • Restricted-location and habitable-room rules
  • Ventilation, enclosure and mounting
  • Protection, isolation and emergency shutdown
  • Signage and shutdown procedure on site
05

Performance

  • Actual generation against realistic site yield
  • String-level output comparison
  • Thermal imaging of modules and connections
  • Monitoring data review where available
  • Underperformance root-cause assessment
06

Documentation

  • Certificate of Compliance / electrical safety certificate
  • As-installed system diagram
  • Commissioning and test records
  • Shutdown procedure and emergency information
  • Warranty documents and installer details

Scope is set to your situation. A battery safety concern, a warranty dispute and a pre-purchase assessment are three different jobs — we scope and price each one on what you actually need, and tell you upfront if an inspection isn't the right answer.

Four steps, no run-around.

Email or call with the symptoms, your address and whatever paperwork you have. An engineer — not a call centre — reads it and tells you whether an inspection will help and what it will cost.

Straight answers

Do you fix what you find?

No — deliberately. GSES does not install, repair or sell equipment. The moment we could profit from the rectification, our findings stop being independent and the report stops being worth anything to you.

We tell you exactly what needs doing so you can get competitive quotes, and we will re-inspect afterwards to confirm the work was done properly.

What does an inspection cost?

It depends on scope and location — a battery safety check, a full compliance and performance assessment and a pre-purchase report are different jobs. Tell us the situation and we'll give you a fixed price before anyone is booked. No obligation, and no charge for the conversation.

Will you inspect a system another company installed?

That is essentially all we do. We have no commercial relationship with installers, and we are engaged by regulators and governments specifically to assess other companies' work without fear or favour. Your installer's identity changes nothing about the report.

Do I need to be home?

Yes, or someone over 18 who can give access. The inspector needs safe access to the roof area, the switchboard, the inverter and any battery. We'll also need to shut the system down briefly to test it safely.

What should I have ready?

Whatever you have: the original quote and invoice, the Certificate of Compliance or electrical safety certificate, any system diagram, monitoring logins, warranty documents, and any correspondence with the installer. If you have none of it, that itself is a finding — bring us the problem anyway.

Can the report be used in a dispute or insurance claim?

Yes. Findings are written against the specific Australian Standard, scheme requirement or manufacturer instruction that has been breached, with photographic evidence. GSES engineers also provide expert witness services on Australian Standards, government program requirements and network requirements where a matter escalates.

How quickly can you get here?

We operate a national inspector network across every state and territory, so lead time depends on your location and how urgent the safety concern is. If you describe something that sounds like an immediate hazard, tell us on the call — and shut the system down at the isolator in the meantime.
